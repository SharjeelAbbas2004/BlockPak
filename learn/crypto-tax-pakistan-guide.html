Crypto Tax in Pakistan: The Complete 2026 Guide

Nobody explains this clearly, so we will — carefully. What Pakistan's tax framework means for your crypto gains, what records to keep, and where the honest uncertainties still are.

1. Where things stand in 2026

Three facts frame everything else:

  • The Virtual Assets Act 2026 (in force 5 March 2026) regulates crypto businesses. It does not, by itself, write the tax code — but it pulls the industry into the formal economy, where tax visibility follows.
  • The State Bank's April 2026 framework lets banks serve PVARA-licensed firms. Banking rails mean traceable PKR flows.
  • A 20% to 30% capital gains tax on crypto profits was on the table ahead of Budget 2026-27. Important: proposals discussed before a budget are not law until enacted. Confirm the final treatment with the FBR or a tax professional before filing.

2. How crypto gains are treated (general principles)

Pakistan's tax system taxes gains on disposal of assets. The conservative, defensible reading is:

  • Profit = sale value minus cost. If you bought 1 BTC at Rs 10,000,000 and sold at Rs 14,000,000, your gain is Rs 4,000,000 — that gain is the taxable base, not the full Rs 14,000,000.
  • Every disposal counts — selling for PKR, swapping BTC for ETH, or spending crypto. Each is a separate event with its own gain or loss.
  • Simply holding (buying and not selling) creates no tax event. Unrealised gains are not taxed.
  • Receiving crypto as income (freelance payments, salary) is income first — record the PKR value on the day received.

4. Record-keeping: your best defence

If the FBR ever asks questions, records are everything. For every transaction, log:

  1. Date and time
  2. Type (buy / sell / swap / receive / transfer)
  3. Amount in crypto and PKR value at the time
  4. Platform or counterparty (exchange, P2P trader, wallet)
  5. Fees paid
  6. Transaction hash or reference ID

Practical tips: export your exchange transaction history every quarter (platforms can freeze or restrict access); screenshot P2P trade confirmations; keep a simple spreadsheet even if you use an app — redundancy is the point.

5. What is still uncertain (honest section)

Any guide that pretends everything is settled is lying to you. Here is what we genuinely do not know yet:

  • The final enacted capital-gains rate for crypto specifically — the 20–30% figure was a pre-budget proposal.
  • Whether holding-period distinctions (short-term vs long-term) will apply to crypto as they do to some other assets.
  • How staking rewards, airdrops and DeFi yields will be classified.
  • Transitional rules for gains made before the new framework.

We update this guide whenever the FBR or PVARA publishes clarifications. When in doubt, the safe move is to declare, document, and consult a professional — not to hide.

6. Frequently asked questions

Is crypto legal in Pakistan in 2026?

Owning and trading crypto as an individual is not an offence. The Virtual Assets Act 2026 regulates crypto businesses and token issuers, not individual holders. However, crypto is not legal tender, and banks follow SBP rules on servicing the sector.

Do I have to pay tax on crypto profits in Pakistan?

Crypto gains are generally expected to fall under capital gains / income tax principles, but the exact enacted treatment should be confirmed with the FBR or a qualified tax professional before you file. A 20 to 30 percent capital gains rate was discussed ahead of Budget 2026-27, but proposals are not law until enacted.

What records should I keep?

Every buy, sell, swap and transfer: date, amounts in crypto and PKR, platform used, fees paid, and wallet addresses. Export your exchange transaction history regularly — platforms can restrict access later.

Are P2P trades taxable?

In principle, yes — a taxable event is about the gain you made, not the venue where you traded. P2P, centralised exchange or on-chain: keep records of all of them.

What happens if I don't declare?

We cannot predict enforcement, but Pakistan now has a dedicated virtual-asset regulator, licensed platforms with reporting obligations, and banking rails coming online. The direction is toward more visibility, not less. Declaring correctly is the safe path.
